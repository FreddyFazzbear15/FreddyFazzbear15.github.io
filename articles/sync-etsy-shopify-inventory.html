How to Sync Etsy and Shopify Inventory Without Overselling

Published October 26, 2026. Facts checked October 26, 2026.

Short answer: Etsy and Shopify don't share inventory on their own. Each one keeps its own stock count, so a sale on one doesn't lower the number on the other. You can keep them aligned three ways: a spreadsheet you update by hand, Shopify-side tools, or a third-party sync app that connects both shops. If you sell print-on-demand through Printful, the bigger risk usually isn't stock counts. It's two copies of every product drifting apart in variants, prices and availability. If you sell handmade, the risk is real: one finished item, two listings, two buyers.

Below is why the drift happens, what each sync method is actually good for, and a checklist you can run today without paying for anything.

Why inventory drifts between your Etsy shop and Shopify store

Your Etsy shop and your Shopify store are two separate systems. A listing on Etsy and a product on Shopify can show the same mug, but to the software they are unrelated records. Nothing links them unless you or a tool does.

Drift comes from a few places:

  • Sales only update one side. Someone buys your last ceramic planter on Etsy. Etsy marks it sold. Shopify still shows one in stock until you change it.
  • Edits only happen in one place. You add a new color on Shopify, raise a price on Etsy, or retire a size in one shop and forget the other.
  • Listings get created separately. If you built your Etsy listings one way and your Shopify products another way, the variants don't line up cleanly. More on that below.
  • Timing. Even with tools, updates aren't always instant. A busy weekend with a few minutes of lag can be enough for a one-of-a-kind item to sell twice.

The fix is less about a magic button and more about deciding which shop is the record of truth, and making every change flow from it.

Three ways to keep Etsy and Shopify in sync

1. A manual spreadsheet

One row per sellable item or variant. Columns for SKU, Etsy listing, Shopify product, quantity on hand, and last updated. When something sells on either side, you update the sheet and then the other shop.

Pros

  • Free, and works today.
  • Forces you to clean up SKUs and variant names, which you need for any other method anyway.
  • You see every number yourself, so nothing changes behind your back.

Cons

  • Only as current as your last update. Sleep through a sale and the other shop is wrong until you wake up.
  • Gets slow past a few dozen items with multiple sizes and colors.
  • Easy to skip during a rush, which is exactly when overselling happens.

Best for: small handmade catalogs, and anyone who wants to understand their numbers before automating.

2. Shopify's own options

Shopify tracks inventory well inside Shopify, and it offers its own app for selling on outside marketplaces, Shopify Marketplace Connect. You may have seen it suggested as the way to connect Etsy.

Check before you rely on that advice. As of October 26, 2026, the Marketplace Connect listing in the Shopify App Store names Amazon, eBay, Target Plus and Walmart as the marketplaces it works with. Etsy isn't on that list. We couldn't confirm when Etsy support changed, so if you set it up a while ago, open the current listing and confirm what it supports for your account.

Pros

  • Free to keep using Shopify as your main inventory record, even if Etsy is updated another way.
  • One admin to look at for Shopify stock.

Cons

  • Based on Shopify's current listing, there's no first-party route that keeps Etsy stock in step automatically.
  • You still need a manual step or another tool for the Etsy side.

Best for: making Shopify your record of truth, paired with a spreadsheet or app for Etsy.

3. Third-party sync apps

Several apps connect an Etsy shop and a Shopify store and push stock changes between them. Some only sync inventory counts. Others also sync orders, prices and listing details.

Pros

  • Automatic updates when an item sells, so less overselling.
  • Saves real time once your catalog has many variants.

Cons

  • Monthly cost, and pricing often climbs with order or listing count.
  • Setup depends on your listings already matching. If your Etsy variants and Shopify variants are structured differently, the app may pair them wrong or not at all.
  • You're trusting a connection to two platforms. If either side changes how it works, sync can quietly stop. Read recent reviews, not just the overall rating.

Best for: sellers with enough volume that manual updates cost more time than the subscription costs money.

Before you pick one, ask: does it match by SKU or by title? Does it handle variants (size and color), or only whole listings? What happens if a listing exists in one shop but not the other? And does it play well with Printful, or will it fight Printful's own updates?

The special problems POD and handmade sellers hit

Printful syncs each store separately

If you use Printful in both shops, you connect Etsy and Shopify to Printful as two separate stores. Printful's help center says there's currently no way to copy a product directly from one store to another. The workaround is to save the product as a template, then add that template to your other store.

That means every product exists twice in Printful, once per shop. Edit the design or retire a variant on your Etsy version, and your Shopify version doesn't change.

Stock isn't really the POD problem. Variant drift is.

Printful treats print-on-demand products as always available unless a variant runs out at its fulfillment centers. Its stock sync, which supports both Shopify and Etsy, checks several times a day and marks synced variants in or out of stock in each connected store. Printful also advises against manually editing store inventory on those products, because it can cause inaccuracies.

So for pure POD, you mostly don't need to count units. What you do need to watch:

  • Variants set up differently per store. A shirt with eight colors on Etsy and six on Shopify. Size labels that read "XL" in one and "X-Large" in the other. These make reporting confusing and break any tool that matches by variant.
  • Unsynced variants. Printful's stock sync only touches variants that are synced to a Printful product. If you created a listing directly in a shop and never matched it in Printful, it won't get out-of-stock updates. Printful's own guide also describes a product as ready for fulfillment once it's linked to its Printful counterpart. Its manual sync lets you match existing listings to the right product and variant.
  • Prices drifting. Printful lets you edit retail prices from its dashboard for both Etsy and Shopify, but per store. Change one, and the other stays where it was.

Handmade: shared materials across listings

Handmade sellers often have the opposite problem. Stock is real and limited, and it's frequently shared. One spool of a hand-dyed yarn might feed three listings. A batch of 20 glass beads might go into earrings, a necklace and a bracelet.

Neither shop understands that. Each listing has its own quantity. Sell five pairs of earrings, and the necklace listing on both shops still claims materials you no longer have.

A workable rule: set each listing's quantity to what you could actually make right now from your current materials, divided across the listings that share them, and recount after every batch. It's conservative, but it's the only way a per-listing count can stay honest.

A checklist you can use today, no app required

  1. Pick your record of truth. One shop or one spreadsheet holds the real number. Everything else copies from it.
  2. Give every variant a SKU. Use the same SKU for the same item in both shops, e.g. MUG-FERN-11OZ.
  3. Line up your variants. Same colors, same sizes, same names in both shops. Remove variants you offer in one but not the other, or add them to both.
  4. For Printful products, check sync status in both stores. In your Printful dashboard, open each store and look for unsynced products or variants. Match them, or remove them from the listing.
  5. Confirm Printful's stock sync is on. Printful's help center lists it under Dashboard → Settings → Store settings → Orders → Sync as you go. Leave it on, and don't hand-edit inventory on those products.
  6. Use templates to copy Printful products between stores. Save the product as a template, then add it to the other store, so both start from the same setup.
  7. Set honest quantities on one-of-a-kind items. If there's only one, the quantity is 1 in both shops, and you check the other shop the moment it sells.
  8. Pick an update time. Twice a day, after your morning and evening order checks, reconcile any handmade or finite stock.
  9. Hold a small buffer on finite stock you update by hand. If you have 4 of something, show 3 in each shop. Underselling by one beats refunding a customer.
  10. Write down what changed. A one-line log (date, SKU, change, which shop) makes mistakes easy to trace.

This won't make overselling impossible. It makes it rare, and when it happens, you'll know why.
